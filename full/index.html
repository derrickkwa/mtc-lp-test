    Your TSH is in range. Your body didn't get the memo.

    Which of these sound familiar?

    Your brain keeps an eye on your thyroid.

    A small gland in your brain checks how much thyroid hormone is in your blood. Then it sends a message to your thyroid: make more, or ease off.

    That message is TSH.

    Animation: a gland in the brain sends a dashed signal, labelled TSH, down to the thyroid in the neck.

    The thyroid answers by sending out T4.

    Mostly T4, about 80% of what it makes. When the brain sees enough in your blood, it eases off. A normal TSH means your brain is satisfied.

    But it doesn't show what happens next, inside your cells.

    Animation: teal T4 orbs flow from the thyroid into the bloodstream. A badge reading TSH in range appears beside them.

    T4 is the raw material.

    Like crude oil. Your cells refine it into T3, the active hormone they actually use for energy, metabolism, mood and temperature.

    Animation: the view moves into a single cell. Teal T4 orbs enter and turn warm blush as they become T3, and the cell glows.

    Sometimes that step stalls.

    More T4 becomes Reverse T3. It fits where T3 should go, but doesn't switch anything on.

    Your blood still looks fine to your brain. Your cells are a different story.

    Animation: more orbs turn muted mauve and dock without lighting up. The cell dims. The TSH in range badge does not change.
